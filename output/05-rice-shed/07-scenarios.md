**Production-side view.** S1 also lowers national paddy CH4 (production basis) from 6,371.2 to 5,850.9 kt CO2e (-8.17%). S2 lowers household footprints only: the rice no longer bought by households moves to the non-household slack in the LP, so national paddy CH4 falls only if production itself falls (cf. the paper's export-rebound caveat, `01-method-summary.md` §4).

**Why the S1 range across units is zero.** Water regime is set nationally (`03-supply-intensity.md`), so S1 scales every producer's intensity by the same factor (0.9183). Every unit changes by the same percentage. A spatially targeted drainage scenario would need water-regime data by si/gun, which Idea 2 does not have.

**Why S2 varies across units.** S2 re-solves the LP with smaller demand. Each unit's purchases fall by the same 11.11%, but the LP re-sources, and the released supply comes off the most distant producers first. Units served by high-intensity producers that drop out gain or lose more than the uniform cut.

**Transcription check.** The FAO line read from the PDF by the script is "Cereals 2% 10% 0.5%, 10% 2% 20%"; the last column (Consumption) equals the transcribed 20%.

## Assumption notes
- **S2 assumes DT_1ED0001 includes household waste.** It treats the per capita consumption in DT_1ED0001 as household acquisition, including household waste. If the survey already measures rice eaten, S2 would instead lower the waste share on top of it, and the demand change would be reported differently.
- **The FAO share is regional, not Korean.** The 20% is FAO's regional default for cereals in Industrialized Asia (Japan, China, Republic of Korea), not a Korean rice-specific measurement.
- **S1 uses the IPCC default ratio.** S1 applies IPCC's default multiple-aeration factor relative to continuous flooding (0.52), as the scenario specification requires. The NID's own ≥ 2× aeration factor is 0.49 (NID 표 5-28); using it would give a slightly larger reduction.
